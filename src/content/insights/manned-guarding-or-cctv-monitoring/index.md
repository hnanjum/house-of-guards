When businesses look at security, the conversation often starts with one question: do we need officers on site, or will cameras do? The honest answer is that they do different jobs, and the right choice depends on your premises, your risks and your budget.

## What manned guarding does well

A security officer on site is a visible, physical presence. That brings things cameras cannot:

- **Deterrence you can see.** A uniformed officer at the entrance changes how people behave.
- **Immediate response.** An officer can challenge someone, stop an incident escalating and give first aid straight away.
- **Access control.** Checking IDs, signing in visitors and contractors, and managing deliveries all need a person.
- **Customer-facing work.** In reception, retail and events, officers often help people as much as they protect property.
- **Judgement.** Officers notice things that are out of place, such as an unlocked door, a suspicious vehicle or a fire risk, and act on them.

The trade-off is cost. Covering a site 24 hours a day, seven days a week needs several officers on rotating shifts.

## What CCTV monitoring does well

Remote CCTV monitoring means trained operators watching your cameras from a control room, usually triggered by motion detection or alarms.

- **Wide coverage** across large or multiple sites for a lower monthly cost.
- **24/7 watching** without staffing every location.
- **Audio challenges.** Many systems let operators speak to intruders through speakers, which stops a lot of incidents early.
- **Evidence.** Recorded footage supports police reports and insurance claims.

The limitation is that cameras cannot physically intervene. Operators can warn, call keyholders and alert the police, but someone still has to attend.

## Questions to help you decide

1. **When are you most at risk?** If incidents happen during opening hours with people around, officers are often the better fit. If the risk is empty sites at night, monitored CCTV can cover it efficiently.
2. **Do you need access control?** If people, visitors or deliveries need checking, you need a person.
3. **How big is the area?** Large yards and multiple buildings are hard for one officer to see. Cameras can watch them all at once.
4. **What happens when something goes wrong?** Decide who responds to an alarm at 3am. If the answer is "nobody", add a response or key holding service.
5. **What is your budget?** Compare the cost of cover against the realistic cost of a loss, including downtime and insurance.

## Often the best answer is both

Many sites get the best value from a combination:

- Officers during working hours for access control and customer contact.
- Monitored CCTV out of hours, with a licensed officer or key holder attending when an alarm is confirmed.

This keeps a human presence where it matters and saves money where cameras can do the job.
