Building sites are an easy target. They change every week, they are often empty at night and over weekends, and they hold valuable plant, tools, fuel and materials. A single break-in can mean lost equipment, delays to the programme and higher insurance costs.

Good site security is rarely one big measure. It is a set of simple layers that make your site harder to get into and less worth the effort. Use the checklist below to review yours.

## 1. The perimeter

- **Solid hoarding or fencing** around the whole site, with no gaps behind cabins or at corners.
- **Secure gates** with strong locks or padlocks, closed and locked outside working hours.
- **Clear signage** stating the site is monitored and patrolled. Signs deter casual thieves.
- **Regular checks** of the fence line for damage, especially after deliveries or bad weather.

## 2. Lighting

- Light the perimeter, gates, compounds and cabins.
- Use dusk-to-dawn or motion-activated lighting so intruders have nowhere to work unseen.
- Make sure lights do not dazzle CCTV cameras or neighbours.

## 3. Plant, tools and materials

- **Store tools** in locked steel containers, not in cabins or vans left on site.
- **Immobilise plant** at the end of every shift, and remove keys from the site.
- **Mark your equipment.** Registering plant under a recognised marking scheme such as CESAR makes it harder to sell on and easier to recover.
- **Order materials just in time** where you can, so high-value stock is not sitting on site overnight.
- **Secure fuel** in locked bowsers or tanks.

## 4. Access control

- One controlled entrance during working hours, with a **sign-in and sign-out** process for workers and visitors.
- Visible ID or site passes for everyone on site.
- A clear rule that deliveries are checked against paperwork on arrival.

## 5. CCTV and alarms

- Cameras covering gates, compounds, containers and high-value areas.
- **Remote monitoring**, so someone is actually watching and can respond when an alarm is triggered, rather than footage only being reviewed the next morning.
- Mobile CCTV towers work well on sites with no fixed power.

## 6. People on the ground

Technology works best with people behind it. Depending on your site, consider:

- **Static guarding** at the gate during working hours to control access.
- **Out-of-hours security** or patrols at night and weekends, when most theft happens.
- **Key holding and alarm response**, so a licensed officer attends when an alarm goes off and you do not have to.

## 7. Paperwork and people

- Keep an up-to-date asset register with serial numbers and photos.
- Brief new starters on site security rules during induction.
- Know your local police contact, and report every incident, even small ones. It builds a record.
